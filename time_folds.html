Time folds

A fold is a refit that moves a lifepath backward: the newest data say the fastest growth happened earlier than the previous fit had projected. This page explains how we count folds, and shows every country's folds.

What a fold looks like

Each refit of the sigmoid curve gives a projected inflection date x₀. Following x₀ from snapshot to snapshot, the lifepath normally moves to the right: either the inflection is pushed further into the future during acceleration, or it stays about where it is. A fold is a step to the left, from one snapshot to the next.

Three ways to count a fold

The same x₀ series can be counted three ways. The paper uses the first two; they answer different questions, so their counts differ.

Calendar rulex₀ moves back by more than 183 days (about six months) between consecutive snapshots, in any phase.
Used in Section 4.3.5: 60 folds in 46 of 114 countries, and 721 folds across 624 of 7,960 authors.
Post-convergence ruleThe same kind of backward move, but only when x₀ already lies within the observation window in both snapshots, so the fit has converged.
Used in Section 4.2.5 and the sensitivity dashboards: no such folds in the padded country runs.
Persistence ruleA calendar-rule fold that also holds: x₀ stays earlier than its pre-fold value in later snapshots, so it is not a blip.
The wording in Section 3.2.3. The reported counts do not apply this extra condition.

Most folds in the point files happen while a lifepath is still accelerating, when x₀ lies beyond the data. The post-convergence rule excludes those by design, which is why "zero post-convergence folds" (Section 4.2.5) and "60 folds in 46 countries" (Section 4.3.5) are both true.

Reproduce the counts

The script recount_folds.py recomputes the paper's numbers from the two point files. Run it in the folder that holds them:

python3 recount_folds.py --dir lifepaths
