Sigmoid-Parameterized Lifepaths

This site is based on the paper “Sigmoid-parameterized lifepaths: A dynamic framework for analyzing research trajectories in scholarly ecosystems” (Buchel, Hedayatifar, Aytac & Tran, Quantitative Science Studies, 2026). Everything here, the lifepaths, the sensitivity dashboards and the code, comes from the analysis reported in that paper.

How does research on carbon footprints grow, mature and change direction? You can explore the research trajectories of countries and authors, each described with one simple curve that is refitted as new publications arrive.

Read the paper ↗ Explore the lifepaths

The idea

Scientific output rarely grows forever. A country or an author typically starts slowly, speeds up, and then levels off. We describe each research trajectory as a cumulative sigmoid (logistic) curve and refit it window by window as new publications are added. Each fit gives an inflection point, the estimated moment of fastest growth, and a phase that says where the entity currently stands on its curve. Following how these values move over time gives every country and author a lifepath.

The data are Web of Science records on carbon-footprint research, from 2007 to mid-2023.

Acceleration
output is still speeding up; the point of fastest growth lies ahead.
Inflection
growth is at or near its fastest.
Deceleration
growth is slowing as the curve approaches its ceiling.
Deactivated
output has essentially stopped.
Fold
a refit that moves the estimated inflection point back (or forward) by more than one window step, for example when a new burst of publications changes the story told by the earlier data.

Sensitivity analyses

A lifepath depends on how the curve is fitted, so we checked whether the conclusions survive reasonable alternative choices. Each dashboard varies the same few settings and shows what changes:

Padding
whether a run of zero-publication months (100) is added before each entity's first publication, so every curve starts from a common baseline, or not.
Fitting rule
aggregate then fit (A) sums publications into 3-, 6- or 12-month windows and fits the curve to those sums, so the window size also sets the resolution of the data; monthly snapshot (B) always fits the monthly series and only records the result at the end of each window, so the window size changes how often results are recorded, not the data.
Window size
3, 6 or 12 months between refits.
First-fit rules
how many documents (0 to 5) and how many months an entity needs before its first fit counts.
